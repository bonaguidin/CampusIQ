## How Your Field Is Evolving

Your target field is shifting, and the direction actually favors you if you prepare deliberately. Read this as path-clarity, not a warning — the goal is to show you where things are heading so you can build toward the durable version of the work.

---

## What's Changing

Embedded and software roles increasingly expect fluency with AI-assisted tooling — code assistants, automated testing, AI-supported debugging. This isn't a threat to entry-level roles so much as a shift in what "entry-level ready" means. Your "moderate" comfort with AI and your informal use of it for code explanation is a fine starting point. The ask isn't to become an AI expert — it's to be able to *articulate* how you use these tools well.

---

## What Stays Durable

The fundamentals don't get automated away — they get more valuable:

- **Debugging judgment** — knowing *why* something breaks, not just that it does.
- **Systems thinking** — understanding how hardware and software interact, the heart of embedded work.
- **Documentation and testing discipline** — the ability to make your reasoning visible and verify your work.

Here's something worth noticing: your professors are already coaching you toward the durable skills. The "show your work" and "document your testing" feedback isn't just about grades — it's building exactly the judgment that survives automation.

---

## How to Talk About It

When you build your documented projects, note where you used AI tools and where you reasoned independently. That articulation — being able to explain your AI fluency, not just demonstrate it — is what employers increasingly probe for, and most students your year can't do it yet. It's a differentiator hiding in plain sight.

---

## Adjacent Paths Worth Watching

As you clarify your direction within Computer Engineering, a few adjacent paths reward the same foundation:

- **Robotics / mechatronics** — heavy overlap with your embedded and hardware interests.
- **Firmware engineering** — deeper on the hardware-software boundary.
- **Hardware-adjacent QA / test engineering** — a strong entry point that values the documentation discipline you're building.

None of these require a decision now. They're worth knowing exist so you can steer toward what pulls you as you gain exposure.
